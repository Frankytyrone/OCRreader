STEP BY STEP THROUGH THE MASS

The Liturgy of the Eucharist (Part 5)

When the priest (or deacon) prepares the Chalice at the Altar the Church requires him to add a very small drop of water to the wine. This is an ancient custom. We have, for example, references to it from the 2$^{nd}$ and 3$^{rd}$ Century writings of several Church Fathers. As the water is added the following words are spoken quietly, almost inaudibly: "By the mystery of this water and wine, may we come to share in the divinity of Christ who humbled himself to share in our humanity." While these words are not heard by the congregation – it might be important for us to understand the words and the act of mixing ('co-mingling') the wine and water that will become the Blood of Christ for us in the Mass.

In the 3$^{rd}$ Century, St. Cyprian, explaining why Wine and Water are used, had this to say: "Because Christ bore us all, in that he bore our sins, we see that by the water, people are signified, while in the wine, indeed, the blood of Christ is shown. And when the water is mixed with the wine in the cup, the people are made one with Christ." What this means is that the wine represents Christ (and his Blood) while the water represents us who are members of Christ Body by virtue of our baptism. The mixing of the water into the wine symbolises the intimate union of the faithful with Jesus and this union is to be so strong and complete that nothing should be able to reverse it. The few drops of water are 'lost' in the wine and can never be retrieved. What is human is swept up in what is Divine. At this point of the Mass, we might take a moment to unite the offering of our lives to Jesus' offering of himself – our hearts united with his. Soon we will be invited to "lift up your hearts". If they are united to the Heart of Jesus, they are lifted to the heights of heaven itself – into the very presence of God the Father.

The further symbolism of this action is that in Christ Jesus the Divine and Human have come together in an everlasting union. Jesus, at his Incarnation (his becoming human in the womb of Mary) brought together the Second Person of the Trinity with a Human Nature. He is fully God and fully Man – in a union of those two natures that will never be separated for all eternity. In his Incarnation, God the Son has united himself with our lowly human nature in an irrevocable act of humility – God becomes one of us – thereby elevating our human nature to an infinitely high status – for one of us (Christ Jesus) sits upon the Throne of the Most High God. He does this so that we too might live and reign with him forever in that same glory. As an early Bishop of the Church (St. Irenaeus) puts it: "The Son of God became the Son of man: so that man, by entering into communion with the Word and thus receiving divine sonship, might become a son of God." This is what the short prayer spoken at the co-mingling of water and wine is alluding to – God humbly took our human nature, and in exchange allows us to share in his exalted Divine nature, what the prayer calls "share in the Divinity of Christ". Not that we become God, but that we become like God, in whose image and likeness we were created.

From Father's Desk

Matthew 14:13-21 - Compassion That Multiplies

In this familiar account of the feeding of the five

thousand, Jesus responds to the crowds not with

frustration but with compassion. Though He had

sought a quiet place after hearing of John the Baptist's

death, He welcomed the people, healed their sick,

and met their hunger.

When the disciples saw only five loaves and two fish,

Jesus saw an opportunity for God's abundance to be

revealed. He invited the disciples to bring what little

they had, blessed it, and used it to feed everyone pre-

sent - with twelve baskets left over.

This passage reminds us that Christ is attentive to both

our spiritual and practical needs. It also encourages

us not to underestimate what God can do with the

small offerings we place in His hands. When we bring

our time, gifts, resources, and willingness to serve,

Jesus can multiply them for the blessing of many.

May we trust Him to provide, serve others with

compassion, and offer what we have

- however small - into His hands.

9Ú LÚNUSA 2026 - NAOÚ DOMHNACH DÉAG - A

Reflection

It's never easy to keep faith, but to hold on to it in stormy times, as many people are going through at present is particularly difficult. Today's Scripture readings encourage us not to lose heart, or give in to despondency, but to trust in the Lord who is with us even when we are unaware of his presence.

'Courage! It is I! Do not be afraid'. These are the same words Jesus speaks to us today as we sail on the uncertain sea of life. He does not promise us a storm free voyage, but he does promise to be always there for us. The example of Peter teaches us that, to stay afloat on the sea of life, we need to fix our gaze on Jesus, not on the surging waves around us, or on the fears and doubts that well up in our hearts.

Today's gospel invites us to deepen our faith and to maintain our focus on Jesus.

In stormy times, there is always a tendency for us to retreat to a cave like Elijah, or to seek out some safe harbour, far from the stormy seas. But clearly that is not what Jesus wants. In today's gospel, Matthew tells us that Jesus 'made his disciples get into the boat and go on ahead of him to the other side' (Mt 14:22). On another occasion, he tells Peter not to hesitate but to 'launch out into the deep' (Lk 5:4). Jesus was calling his disciples, as he is calling us today, to join him in the greatest adventure known to human history: the realisation of God's dream of a world transformed by the power of love. And, irrespective of what storms we encounter, we shall not fail, because Jesus is always with us.
