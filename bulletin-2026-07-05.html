Thank you

Ahead of my farewell Masses this weekend, I want to acknowledge the massive help and assistance I received from Fr. Matt McInerney over the last 22 years. Fr. Matt called and offered his services at the very beginning of my tenure and simply said, "It is good to keep a foot in the game!" During that time he has been a wonderful source of wisdom, advice and company. For 22 years we met for a coffee every Friday unless something big got in our way, and his humour, observations and kindness have been a deep source of living water from a well that flowed freely in our parish of which all could partake. I roughly worked out that over this time, we consumed 142 gallons of coffee together while discussing the ins and outs of Parish life!

His gentle steering of things Liturgical, his affinity with the youth of the parish in the Do this in Memory programmes and with the altar servers was a beautiful thing to behold. His thoughtful words inspired us all during his short homilies. His silent witness to a prayerful life was so evident as he spent a half hour reading his breviary before Mass was inspiring and humbling at the same time. He still does it! I hope Father Vincent makes a good cup of coffee, or maybe I don't, so that Fr. Matt might decide to make his way to Dungloe to satisfy his predilection for the midnight nectar that is a cup of Joe. Thanks Fr. Matt for your service to the parish, and for your friendship over the years.

Reflection

The Lord said to me, 'Come to me.'

But I said, I'm not worthy.'

'Come to me,' he repeated. And I said, 'I'm afraid.'

'Come to me.' 'I'm too proud.'

'Come to me.' 'But I've no appointment.'

'Come to me.' 'But I can't afford the time right now.'

'Come to me.' With that I fell silent.

Then he said, 'Come ... sit down ... take the load

off your feet.'

'Sit here as in the shade of the tree.

'Refresh yourself as at a running stream.

'Here you will find rest. Here you will find peace.

'And your yoke will become easy, and your

burden light.'

- Flor McCarthy, SDB

QUADJUCENTENNIAL [125th ANNIVERSARY] CATHEDRAL OF SAINTS EUNAN & COLUMBIA, LETTERENNY

To mark the 125th Anniversary of the consecration of the Cathedral of Saints Eunan and Columbia, Letterkenny, the Holy See has granted a Plenary Indulgence under the usual conditions. It may be obtained as follows:

The Plenary Indulgence may be obtained under the usual conditions by any of the faithful who, rightly disposed, participate in the liturgical life of the Cathedral with the intention of gaining the Indulgence, beginning with the Mass of Chrism on Holy Thursday of this year and concluding with the feast of the Epiphany, 6th January 2027. The same Indulgence may be obtained by the sick and others who, unable to leave their homes, participate spiritually in the same liturgical services by electronic means.

The Indulgence may be gained under the usual conditions by all who piously come on pilgrimage to the Cathedral in the aforementioned period with that purpose in mind.

The same Indulgence is extended to those who wish to obtain it by spending at least one hour in Eucharistic Adoration at the St. Pius X Chapel of Perpetual Adoration in the grounds of the Cathedral.

To obtain an indulgence, the usual conditions of detachment from all sin, Sacramental Confession, Holy Communion, and prayer for the intentions of the Pope must be met within 7 days.

Why Sunday Matters

Our invitation to reflect on Sunday Mass is presented through the lens of Synodality - Communion, Participation, and Mission - reminding us that the Eucharist is not just a personal encounter with Christ but also a communal experience that unites us all as the People of God. Mass is not just a personal act of devotion; it is a communal act of worship. We go to God together. The Sunday Mass is where the Church is most visibly alive, and where every member of the community plays an essential role. Each person's participation is needed for the Church to truly live.

We are deeply thankful to priests, ministers and all those who gather to celebrate Mass. Special gratitude goes to those involved in liturgical ministries, such as Ministers of the Word, of Holy Communion, Music Ministers, Sacristians, and Altar Servers. Encouraging the return of young people as Altar Servers is especially important and should be actively supported by their families and parish.

(Irish Catholic Bishops' Conference, Why Sunday Matters, 2025)

Reflection

Today's reading from Matthew's Gospel comes after a discourse in which Jesus reproaches people who have witnessed his mighty deeds yet still lack belief. In this context, today's Gospel explains the reason for this unbelief and reveals what is necessary for faith. Today's Gospel also continues to enhance our understanding of discipleship as last week's Gospel did.

Jesus first prays in thanksgiving to God who has made himself known to Jesus' disciples. He praises God who has made himself known to the 'little ones' and not to the wise and learned. As in other recent readings from Matthew's Gospel, a contrast is made here between the unbelieving Pharisees, who are wise and learned, and the faithful disciples, tax collectors, and sinners with whom Jesus keeps company.

The second part of this reading calls to our attention the unity between the Father and the Son. God has made himself known through Jesus, and in knowing Jesus, we come to know the Father. In Jesus' life and in his person, God reveals himself to us.

*'The Lord make his face to shine upon you and give you peace'.*
