The First Five Saturdays - To begin from the month of August

Then our Lady said to Lucy at Fatima:

"Look, my daughter, at my Heart surrounded with thorns with which ungrateful men pierce me at every moment by their blasphemies & ingratitude...

You at least, must try to console me & say I promise to assist at the hour of death, with the graces necessary for salvation, all those who on the first Saturday of five consecutive months, shall confess, receive Holy Communion, recite five decades of the Rosary & keep me company for fifteen minutes while meditating on the fifteen mysteries of the Rosary, with the intention of making reparation."

How to practice this Devotion

Our Lady asks us on five successive First Saturdays of the month to;

1 Go to Confession

This Sacramental Confession may be made within eight days, or in case of difficulty even later, provided we are in a state of grace when we receive Our Lord.

2 Receive Holy Communion

3 Pray five decades of the Rosary

Our lady asks us to say after each decade the prayer; O Jesus, forgive us our sins, save us from the fire of hell. Lead all souls to heaven, especially those in most need of Your mercy.

4 Keep Our Lady company for fifteen minutes thinking with her about the mysteries of the Rosary.

5 Have the intention of making reparation to the Immaculate Heart of Mary.

A Creed to Live By

Nancye Sims

Don't undermine your worth by comparing yourself with others.

It is because we are different that each of us are special.

Don't set your goals by what other people deem important.

Only do what is best for you.

Don't take for granted the things closest to your heart.

Cling to them as you would your life, for without them life is meaningless.

Don't let life slip through your fingers by living in the past or in the future.

By living one day at a time you live all days of your life.

Don't give up when you still have something to give.

Nothing is really over

until the moment you stop trying.

Don't be afraid to admit

that you are less then perfect.

It is the fragile thread that binds us to each other.

Don't be afraid to encounter risks.

It is by taking chances

that we learn how to be brave.

Don't shut love out of your life by saying it is impossible.

The fastest way to lose love is to hold to it tightly,

and the best way to keep love is to give it wings.

Don't dismiss your dreams.

To be without dreams is to be without hope, to be without hope is to be without purpose.

Don't run through life so fast

that you forget not only where you have been, but also where you are going.

Life is not a race,

but a journey to be savoured each step of the way.

This weekend Gospel gives three short parables that all point to the same truth: the Kingdom of Heaven is so valuable that it is worth everything. In the treasure hidden in a field and the pearl of great price, Jesus shows that a person who truly discovers God's kingdom will joyfully give up lesser goods to gain what is infinitely greater. The kingdom is not just one more option in life; it is the great treasure, the pearl worth every sacrifice.

The parable of the dragnet adds a warning and a promise. God's kingdom is present in the world now, but at the end of time there will be a final separation between what is faithful to God and what is not. Jesus' teaching says that the kingdom is both a gift to be welcomed and a reality that asks for a decision: we must choose it, remain faithful to it, and live in a way that reflects it.

For parish life, the message is simple and practical: put Christ first. The kingdom is entered by faith, love, forgiveness, and perseverance, and it calls us to generosity, vigilance, and compassionate service. Jesus teaches that God's reign is the arrival of divine goodness and love in our midst, and that those who embrace it become "kingdom builders" in the Church and in the world.

The Liturgy of the Eucharist (Part 3)

The essential elements for the Mass are Bread and Wine – for without these we cannot have the Eucharist. Nothing can substitute these two elements for the Eucharist was instituted by Christ himself and therefore it is inadmissible and would be invalid to seek to use any other material (such as biscuits and cola) under whatever reasoning might cause someone to think this a valid alternative. It must be wine; it must be bread – and these two in their most basic form. The wine used is from vineyards that make wine exclusively for Church purposes and has no additives whatsoever. Such a vineyard would need certification from Church authorities as to their authenticity and brewing practices. Likewise, the bread is simple wheat bread with no additives – flour and water are the only ingredients used. The bread too is made by those specially commissioned to do so.

Bread and Wine, apart from the fact that they were chosen by Jesus himself to be the matter by which he will affect the wondrous change that makes them his Body and Blood, are eminently appropriate for the Sacred Purpose of the Mass. Pope Benedict XVI taught several times about the Bread and Wine used for the Eucharist. He reminded us that Bread is a symbol of hard work and toil – the difficulties of daily living, while wine is a symbol of joy and celebration. As such they represent the hardships and joys of the faithful who offer them to be placed on the altar. In a sense the offertory is our way of saying to the Lord: “Here we present to you the difficulties, struggles and efforts of our lives and we offer you also all that is good and joyful and going well. Take these as representations of our whole lives and do with them what you will.” In this moment too, we can spiritually place on the Altar, all in our lives that needs God’s grace and blessing. As we place the bread and wine on the Altar – we await from our Good God good things. And what does he do with those gifts of bread and wine? Jesus takes up these symbols of our lives and our offering into his offering of himself to the Father for the salvation of the whole world. And the Father returns these gifts to us at Communion time – not as mere symbols of his grace and blessing coming through Jesus – he will give us Jesus himself in Holy Communion – Jesus, the source of every grace and blessing we will ever need.

“This humble and simple gesture is actually very significant: in the bread and wine that we bring to the altar, all creation is taken up by Christ the Redeemer to be transformed and presented to the Father. In this way we also bring to the altar all the pain and suffering of the world, in the certainty that everything has value in God’s eyes.” Pope Benedict XVI

Reflection

This week, we pray to live each day with the wisdom to know what truly matters, and to let go of anything that holds us back. We should live every moment in view of our precious goal. Most of the time, we are chasing false treasures such as money, power, social status, or worldly pleasures. Thus, the really valuable 'pearl' of sharing in God's life through Jesus here on earth and later in Heaven, is never found. Let us always remember that Heaven is within the reach of all of us who try to do the will of God, following the ordinary vocations of life and enjoying this world's joys and pleasures within the framework of God's Commandments. When we do this, we are already living in the Kingdom of Heaven while we are still on earth.

We need to take all precautions to keep our greatest treasure safe. We will be able to keep safe the treasure of our personal relationship with Jesus by accepting him every day as our God and Saviour, by allowing God to have total control over our lives through our loving obedience to His will, by asking Him daily for the strengthening and guidance of His Holy Spirit, by talking to Him daily in prayer, by listening to Him in our meditative reading of the Bible, by getting reconciled to Him and others every day, asking pardon and forgiveness for our sins, by offering Him our lives on the altar during the Holy Mass, and by allowing Him to nourish our souls frequently, by receiving Jesus in Holy Communion. 'Lord, help us to share the joy of following you, wherever and however we can.'
